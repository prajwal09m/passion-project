## Responses to H-10 Packet Questions

### Q1 — Exchangeability / Sign Symmetry

**Status: NOT SUPPORTED — ASSUMPTION REQUIRED**

Under the intended null, week-level units are exchangeable in arm label **only if** the sharp null holds: within every week, the joint distribution of the two arms' score vectors is invariant to swapping arm labels.

This is **not a design feature** (there is no randomization to points of treatment). It is a **distributional symmetry assumption** about how the two models' score vectors relate within weeks.

**Why it's unestablishable by local artifact:**
- The arms are deterministic fitted models evaluated on identical events
- Exchangeability fails to the extent that arms differ in score shape (scale, skew, heteroskedasticity) in ways not reducible to a monotone transformation of a shared latent
- Shared-training dependence (overlapping OOF fits) and calibration differences plausibly create shape asymmetries
- No diagnostic can convert this assumption into a verified fact — it is fundamentally unverifiable in principle

**C4 review empirical bounds:**
- Under exchangeable null: 50/1000 rejections (0.050, exact calibration)
- Under affine scale asymmetry (×1.6): 55/1000 (0.055, not significant, p = 0.25)
- Under heteroskedastic asymmetry (σ⁺_B=1.5): 60/1000 (0.060, not significant, p = 0.087)

**Assessment:** The assumption is unverifiable but the empirical bounds from C4 review suggest mild degradation under tested asymmetry shapes. This is acceptable **only** as an owner-accepted limitation.

### Q2 — Temporal Dependence

**Status: SUPPORTED — HOLM TOLERATES ARBITRARY DEPENDENCE**

Weeks are adjacent in time and share training data. Under the intended null, this creates **maximal dependence** between family members (they share events, labels, training rows, and market weeks).

**Why this is acceptable:**
- Holm step-down controls FWER under **arbitrary dependence** given super-uniform marginal p-values
- This is a theoretical property of the Holm procedure (it does not require independence of tests)
- The marginal super-uniformity is the only inferential bottleneck (addressed in Q3)

**Shared-training dependence:**
- Overlapping OOF fits create dependence between tests
- This is already captured by the "maximal dependence" case that Holm tolerates
- No dependence-aware randomization is required

**Assessment:** Holm's theoretical guarantee under arbitrary dependence makes temporal/shared-training dependence acceptable. No block-level or dependence-aware randomization modification is required.

### Q3 — Super-Uniformity of Marginal P-Values

**Status: SUPPORTED WITH DISCLOSURE — MILD ELEVATION ACCEPTED**

Under the actual null (including the asymmetry classes actually produced by the estimators), the marginal p-values are **not exactly super-uniform**.

**C4 review findings:**
- Under exchangeable null: 0.050 (exact calibration)
- Under affine scale asymmetry: 0.055 (Clopper-Pearson 95% upper bound: 0.071)
- Under heteroskedastic asymmetry: 0.060 (Clopper-Pearson 95% upper bound: 0.077)

**Monte Carlo error:**
- At N = 1000 trials, the standard error for p = 0.05 is sqrt(0.05 × 0.95 / 1000) ≈ 0.0069
- The observed elevations (0.005, 0.010) are within Monte Carlo error
- The upper bounds (0.071, 0.077) are above nominal but not dramatically so

**Is this acceptable for multiplicity-corrected decisions?**
- Yes, for degradation-only claims about already-promoted candidates
- The elevation is mild (≤ +1.0 percentage point estimate)
- Not individually significant at N = 1000 (p = 0.25, 0.087)
- Consistent sign across asymmetry classes suggests a small real effect

**Assessment:** Mild elevation is acceptable **only** as an owner-accepted limitation. The method remains valid for its intended use (degradation findings behind a firewall).

### Q4 — Adequacy of the Null Calibration

**Status: SUPPORTED WITH DISCLOSURE — LIMITATIONS ACKNOWLEDGED**

The two asymmetry classes tested in C4 review are:
1. Affine scale asymmetry (multiplying one arm's scores by 1.6)
2. Heteroskedastic asymmetry (different variance for positives in one arm)

**Are these sufficient?**
- They probe two plausible violation mechanisms (scale difference, heteroskedasticity)
- They bound degradation at the α = 0.05 level (not at the Holm decision level α/m ≈ 0.001–0.002)
- Tail behavior under asymmetry remains unmeasured

**What null scenarios are missing:**
- Autocorrelated arm differences (not simulated)
- Regime-dependent variance (not simulated)
- More complex shape asymmetries (not simulated)
- Joint dependence structure across weeks (not simulated)

**Why this is acceptable:**
- No simulation battery can exhaustively cover all possible violations
- The tested scenarios are constructive and plausible
- The bounds are honest about what was measured
- The unmeasured tail behavior is a disclosed limitation

**Assessment:** The calibration is **sufficient for approval with disclosed limitations**. Missing scenarios are acknowledged; the bounds are honest. This is acceptable only as an owner-accepted limitation.

### Q5 — Monte-Carlo Resolution Near Holm Thresholds

**Status: SUPPORTED WITH DISCLOSURE — COARSENESS ACCEPTED**

With M = 1000:
- Minimum representable p = 1/1001 ≈ 0.001
- Borderline decisions are resolved with 25–48% probability (biased toward non-rejection)

**C4 review resolution table (for m = family size):**

| m | first threshold α/m | max count that rejects | P(reject | true p = α/m) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12 | 0.00417 | 3 | 0.401 |
| 24 | 0.00208 | 1 | 0.384 |
| 36 | 0.00139 | 0 | 0.249 |

**Is the decision reliable enough?**
- For m = 36 (regime family), a member must produce the minimum p (1/1001) to survive the first step
- Borderline members (true p near threshold) are decided with low probability (25–48%)
- This makes the test **conservative-leaning and coarse** where families are largest

**Is a larger M required?**
- Statistical validity is unaffected (discreteness is conservative)
- Power and decision stability are the costs
- Increasing M would require a protocol amendment (M is pinned by the protocol)

**Assessment:** Coarseness is acceptable **only** as an owner-accepted limitation. The method remains valid but has reduced power near thresholds. No protocol amendment is required unless the owner decides to increase M.
